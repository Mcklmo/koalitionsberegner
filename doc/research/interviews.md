# Stage 3 — Interviews

8–12 conversations. **Past behaviour only.** Nobody is asked whether they would
use this; they are asked what they did last time.

## The script

The question that carries the whole thing:

> Last election — when you wanted to know whether party X and party Y together
> had a majority, what did you actually do?

Then, in order, and only as follow-ups to what they said:

1. What did you use? (Show me, if you still have it.)
2. What did you do with the answer — keep it, send it to someone, argue with it?
3. What was annoying about it?
4. When did you last look something like that up? (Pins it to a real date,
   which is how you find out whether the need is campaign-shaped or
   coalition-talks-shaped.)
5. Who else do you know who does this more than you?

No link in the first message. The link goes out only after they have described
what they did last time — otherwise the rest of the conversation is a reaction
to the product rather than a record of their behaviour.

## Who, and why

| Segment | N | Why them | Reaching them, free |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Data / election-desk journalists at **small** outlets | 3–4 | The only segment with both reach and money, and `GET /api/elections` is already embeddable with `ALLOWED_ORIGINS`. A small outlet in a country with no calculator has the need and no budget to build one | Email, with a working link to *their* election prepared first |
| Civics and social-studies teachers | 2–3 | Steady, off-cycle demand, and the strongest material for an NLnet or Prototype Fund application | Subject-teacher groups, a school's own contact page |
| Engaged voters found in Stage 2's flagged comments | 3–4 | The people whose behaviour the product is built on | Reddit DM, disclosed as research |
| A psephologist or political-science researcher | 1–2 | Will tell you which seat-allocation rule is wrong before a journalist finds out | University department pages |

## What each answer would mean

- *"I looked at the newspaper's calculator."* The incumbent is the competition,
  and the wedge is only the elections outlets do not cover — sub-national and
  small-country. Narrows the roadmap sharply.
- *"I counted the seats myself / opened a spreadsheet."* The README's pitch is
  literally true. Entrypoint is search plus community answers.
- *"I asked in a thread."* Stage 2's channel is the channel.
- *"I'd embed it if it had my election."* Build the demo for that outlet, not
  more API.
- *"I only cared on election night."* The tail the pageview data shows is
  Wikipedia's, not the product's; re-plan around the 72 hours after the vote.
